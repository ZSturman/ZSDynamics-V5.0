Traffic quality

Traffic was mixed: 5 likely human, 7 automated, 1 uncertain.

Strongest automation signals: no scroll, pointer, key or touch activity (5 sessions); identical page sequences repeated across sessions (4 sessions); many page loads within a minute (2 sessions).

Likely-human sessions showed scrolling, pointer or touch input, realistic page timing or sustained active time. That supports, but does not prove, a person was present.

GA4 (49) and the session stream (14) count differently: blockers and Global Privacy Control affect them differently, and they define sessions differently.

Problems and opportunities

Traffic spike: 49 sessions vs a 7-day average of 8 a day
Compare with the traffic-quality split before treating it as new interest.
All 49 GA4 sessions had no source
The session stream saw a referrer or campaign for 5 sessions, while GA4 reported mostly direct traffic. The two systems observe different sets of sessions.
27 sessions came from cities that host large cloud data centres
Council Bluffs, Iowa alone had 21 sessions. Location is never used to classify a session — people live there too — but it is worth comparing with the traffic-quality evidence.
Better Workspace: 10 resource clicks vs 3 detail-page views
7 of the clicks happened outside the detail page — /projects (5), /better-workspace (2) — on project cards and product pages, so the counts are not contradictory.
13 desktop sessions used a 768–1023 px window
Common browser-automation tools open 800×600 windows by default. Supporting evidence only; a narrow desktop window is also normal.
